01 — Introduction

Our commitment to you

Tempris Technology Pte. Ltd. ("Tempris", "we", "us") builds a Continuous Threat Exposure Management (CTEM) platform used by regulated financial institutions in Singapore. We hold ourselves to the same security standard we provide to our clients.

We believe responsible security research makes the internet safer. We actively welcome researchers who find vulnerabilities in our systems and choose to report them to us responsibly. If you have found something that looks like a security issue, please tell us.

This policy is our commitment to you: if you follow these guidelines, we will not pursue legal action, we will acknowledge your work, and we will fix what you find.

This policy is published in the spirit of the disclose.io framework and meets the requirements for Full Safe Harbor with Coordinated Disclosure (Level 5).

02 — Safe Harbor

We will not come after you

The following Safe Harbor terms apply to all security research conducted under this policy. These terms are based on the disclose.io core VDP terms and satisfy all four mandatory Safe Harbor tenets.

🛡️

Authorisation Against Anti-Hacking Laws

We authorise your security research activities and will not initiate legal action under the Computer Misuse and Cybersecurity Act (CMCA) or equivalent laws for good-faith testing within this policy's scope.

🔓

Exemption from Anti-Circumvention Laws

We will not pursue claims under anti-circumvention provisions (including the Copyright Act 2021) for research conducted in good faith in accordance with this policy.

📋

Exemption from TOS / AUP Violations

We exempt you from restrictions in our Terms of Service that would otherwise prohibit security testing, provided your research is conducted within scope and in good faith.

🤝

Good-Faith Acknowledgement

We recognise that good-faith security research is a valuable contribution. We will treat your report fairly, acknowledge it promptly, and work with you collaboratively.

FORMAL SAFE HARBOR STATEMENT

We consider security research conducted under this policy to be authorised activity and will not initiate or recommend legal action against you for activities that comply with these terms.

If legal action is initiated by a third party against you in connection with activities conducted under this policy, we will take steps to make it known that your activities were conducted in compliance with this policy.

Tempris cannot bind the actions of third parties. If you are subject to legal action, contact us immediately at lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg and we will provide a written statement confirming your compliance with this policy.

This safe harbor applies only to activities conducted within the scope defined in Section 04 and in accordance with the Rules of Engagement defined in Section 05. Out-of-scope testing or testing that causes harm to third parties is not covered.

Singapore Law Context. This policy operates within the framework of Singapore law, including the Computer Misuse and Cybersecurity Act (CMCA), the Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA), and the MAS Technology Risk Management Guidelines (TRM). Researchers are expected to comply with all applicable laws. This policy does not constitute legal advice. If you are uncertain whether a specific activity is permitted, ask us before proceeding at lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg.
03 — Programme Structure

Two tiers, one policy

Our programme operates at two levels. Both tiers are covered by the same Safe Harbor terms and rules of engagement.

TIER 1 Public VDP — Always Open

Anyone may report a vulnerability to us at any time through lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg or via our submission form. No invitation required. No screening. This is our always-on disclosure channel — it exists so no legitimate finder ever hits a dead end.

Tier 1 covers the entire Tempris attack surface. If you discover a vulnerability in any Tempris-operated system, we want to hear about it.

TIER 2 Invited Testing Programme — sandbox.tempris.tech

A limited number of invited researchers are authorised to conduct active, hands-on testing against sandbox.tempris.tech — our dedicated testing environment. Invited researchers receive expanded scope, direct communication with the Tempris security team, and eligibility for Hall of Fame recognition and Letters of Recommendation.

To request an invitation, email lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg with a brief summary of your background, any prior responsible disclosures or CVEs, and what you would like to test. We review all requests.

04 — Scope

What you may test

The following assets are explicitly in scope. We encourage broad, inclusive testing — the full attack surface is the right scope for a true VDP.

Asset Type Tier Notes

tempris.tech Marketing / Public Web Tier 1 (Public) Report only — no active testing without invitation.

Tempris Open Source Code Source Code Tier 1 (Public) Any publicly available Tempris repositories.

Out of Scope

The following systems and activities are out of scope. Testing them may cause harm to others and is not authorised under this policy.

  • Production client environments and any system not operated by Tempris
  • Third-party services and infrastructure (cloud providers, payment processors, DNS registrars)
  • Social engineering attacks against Tempris employees, contractors, or clients
  • Physical security testing (offices, data centres, hardware)
  • Denial of Service (DoS / DDoS) against any Tempris system
  • Automated scanning tools that generate excessive traffic volumes
  • Accessing, exfiltrating, or retaining real user data beyond what is necessary to demonstrate the vulnerability
  • Testing against systems of clients or partners without their explicit written permission
  • Email phishing, vishing, or smishing directed at Tempris personnel
Scope ambiguity. If you are unsure whether a particular system or activity falls within scope, ask us first. We would rather clarify in advance than have you stop a valuable finding or, worse, take an action that neither of us intended. Email us at lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg before proceeding.
05 — Rules of Engagement

How to conduct good-faith research

These rules protect you legally, protect our clients, and ensure we can fix what you find. They are not designed to limit discovery — they are designed to keep everyone safe while you do it.

You must

  • Report the vulnerability to us before disclosing it to anyone else
  • Provide sufficient detail to reproduce the vulnerability (steps, payloads, screenshots or screen recordings where applicable)
  • Stop testing immediately if you encounter what appears to be real production data or client data — report what you found without further access
  • Keep all vulnerability details confidential until we have confirmed a fix or until the coordinated disclosure period has expired, whichever is sooner (see Section 08)
  • Use test accounts or data you have created yourself wherever possible
  • Act in good faith — your goal is to improve security, not to cause harm or extract value

You must not

  • Exploit a vulnerability beyond the minimum necessary to demonstrate its existence
  • Access, copy, modify, delete, or retain data beyond what is needed to prove the issue
  • Disrupt or degrade the availability of any Tempris system or service
  • Perform Denial of Service testing of any kind
  • Use automated scanners without prior written approval from Tempris
  • Test in a way that targets, involves, or harms third parties
  • Share details of any found vulnerability with another party before coordinated disclosure is agreed
  • Use the access gained through vulnerability research for any purpose other than reporting to Tempris
  • Conduct physical testing, social engineering, or attempt to obtain credentials through non-technical means
If in doubt, stop and ask. Contacting us at lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg before taking an uncertain action is always the right move and will never be held against you. A researcher who asks first is a researcher we trust.

What to include in your report

  • Vulnerability type — e.g. SQLi, XSS, IDOR, SSRF, auth bypass, logic flaw
  • Affected asset — URL, endpoint, API route, or component
  • Steps to reproduce — numbered, with enough detail for our team to reproduce independently
  • Proof of concept — screenshots, screen recording, or code snippet demonstrating the issue
  • Impact assessment — what could an attacker do with this? What data or systems are at risk?
  • Suggested remediation — optional but appreciated, especially for complex findings
  • Your contact details — so we can reach you for follow-up and recognition

You do not need to calculate a CVSS score. We will score the finding internally and share our assessment with you. If you disagree with our severity rating, we welcome a discussion — we may be missing context you have.

Severity definitions

SeverityCVSS RangeExample findings
CRITICAL 9.0 – 10.0 RCE, authentication bypass granting admin access, mass data exfiltration, account takeover at scale
HIGH 7.0 – 8.9 SQLi, stored XSS, SSRF with internal access, IDOR to sensitive data, privilege escalation
MEDIUM 4.0 – 6.9 Reflected XSS, CSRF on sensitive actions, insecure direct object reference (limited impact), information disclosure
LOW 0.1 – 3.9 Missing security headers, verbose error messages, low-risk misconfigurations, best-practice deviations

Out-of-scope finding types

The following finding types are generally not eligible for recognition unless paired with a demonstrated, realistic attack path:

  • Missing rate limiting (without demonstrated account enumeration or brute-force feasibility)
  • Self-XSS (requires victim to execute code in their own browser)
  • Clickjacking without demonstrated sensitive action
  • Password or account policy (complexity requirements, lockout thresholds)
  • Unconfirmed reports from automated scanners without manual validation
  • Theoretical vulnerabilities without evidence of exploitability
  • Version disclosure of libraries or frameworks without demonstrated vulnerability
  • SPF, DMARC, DKIM issues without demonstrated email spoofing impact
08 — Coordinated Disclosure

How we handle your report over time

We follow a Coordinated Disclosure model. We will work with you to confirm, remediate, and publicly disclose findings on an agreed timeline. We will never ask you to suppress a valid finding indefinitely.

Day 0
Submission received. Automated acknowledgement sent immediately. Human acknowledgement within 5 business days.
Day 1–10
Triage. We confirm reproducibility, assign internal severity, and contact you if we need additional information. We share our severity rating with you and invite your input.
Day 10–30
Remediation. Critical and High findings are prioritised for immediate patching. We provide status updates at least every 14 days during active remediation.
Day 30–90
Complex findings. For findings requiring significant architectural changes, we will agree a remediation timeline with you. We commit to a maximum of 90 days from submission to fix or public disclosure, whichever is sooner.
Day 90
Disclosure deadline. If a fix has not been applied within 90 days of submission, you are free to disclose the finding publicly, regardless of our fix status. We will notify you of this proactively if we anticipate it. We will never use legal threats to prevent disclosure of a legitimate finding after this period.
Post-fix
Coordinated public disclosure. Once a fix is deployed, we will coordinate with you on public disclosure timing, credit language, and Hall of Fame listing. Default: 7-day notice period before public disclosure.
We will always give you credit. We will never publish details of your finding publicly without your knowledge and without naming you (unless you explicitly request anonymity). Your report belongs to you; we are grateful you shared it with us.
09 — Regulatory Context

Singapore law and our client obligations

Because Tempris serves regulated financial institutions, the following context is important for researchers to understand before testing.

Personal Data Protection Act (PDPA)

Singapore's PDPA applies to any personal data you may encounter during testing. If you encounter what appears to be personal data belonging to Tempris clients or their end users, stop immediately, do not access further, and report what you observed via lohsherie@yahoo.com.sg. Do not copy, retain, or transmit personal data under any circumstances. Doing so may constitute a PDPA breach independent of this policy's safe harbor.

MAS Technology Risk Management (TRM) Guidelines

Tempris's platform is designed for use by MAS-regulated financial institutions. Our sandbox environment contains only synthetic test data and has no connection to any client production environment or real financial data. Testing in the sandbox poses no risk to client data.

Computer Misuse and Cybersecurity Act (CMCA)

The CMCA criminalises unauthorised access to computer systems in Singapore. This policy constitutes explicit authorisation for testing within scope. Testing outside scope is not authorised and may constitute a CMCA offence. If you are uncertain whether a particular action is within scope, ask us first.

ISO/IEC 42001:2023 — AI Governance

Tempris's platform incorporates AI decision-making components. Reports relating to AI governance risks — including model manipulation, prompt injection, or AI-driven access control bypass — are in scope and treated as high-priority findings.
